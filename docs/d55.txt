         Sir Alexander Johnstone, one of the Dominion's most
    distinguished citizens, served the Institute and Museum selflessly
    for many years. At different times he held the offices of Member

       Spitfire
            Thanks to generous help given by the Shipping Companies
       (Con fe rence Lines) and by the New Zealand Government, the
       Spitfire Fighter presented by the British Air Ministry has been
       brought to New Zealand and placed on display.

       Marshal Sir Keith Pa rk, G.C.B., K.B .E., D .S.O., M.C., the Hon .
       S. G. Smith, the Hon . R. M. Algie, Mr. J. Seabrook and Mr. J. B.
       O'Loghlen.
            A special ceremony of dedication was held. an innovation
       being the making of a tape-record ing which should prove of
       definite historic interest in the future.
            I n view of the part played by the Spitfire in the Battle of
       Britain, as in so many other engagements in the second world war,
       it is singularly fitting that one of these famous planes should be
       placed on permanent exhibition in the War Memorial Museum.

       School Service
           The provision by the Education Department of an additional
       education officer has proved most welcome, and has enabled the
       Museum to cope with a much greater number of school visits .

       Donations
            As in previous yea rs many welcome and valuable gifts have
       been made to the Museum du ring the year just ended. Special
       mention should be made of the generous action of Sir Carrick
       Robertson in presenting the sum of £600 as an endowment
       for research work on offshore islands, and of a much appreciated
       gift of £ 100 from Sir William Goodfellow for the purchase of
       early English ceramics.

       Western Springs
